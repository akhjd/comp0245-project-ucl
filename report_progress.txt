PROGRESS REPORT - expert and MLP for moving obstacles
Last updated: 2 Nov 2026 (deadline about 8 Nov)

GOAL
Build an expert that drives the base to a parking spot and reaches a target while avoiding static and
moving obstacles. An MLP will then be trained to imitate it from lidar and robot state.
Order: fix the expert first, then train the MLP.

CURRENT STATE
- Expert: RRTExpert in obstacle_env.py, used with --rrt. Without --rrt, the old SteerAround expert runs.
- Knows exact obstacle positions, sizes, velocities and turning points from the simulator. Uses no lidar.
- Plans with a time-aware RRT, using moves the base can really make (arcs, spins, reverse, wait).
- Latest fair result: 35/36 new floor plans with 3 dots. Usually collision-free, not guaranteed.
- Also handles interior walls, furniture and several dots touched in turn.
- Dataset: 1625 clean expert runs in five cases, about 5.5 million rows.
- MLP: first model trained without DAgger (runs/mlp1/best.pt, validation R2 0.949). It fails when driving: 0/7 runs, 0/11 dots.
- DAgger with the RRT expert is written (dagger_mlp.py) and has not been run yet.

DECISIONS AND WHY
1. Added moving obstacles to the simulator.
   Why: the original obstacles were all static. The old expert collided with a crossing obstacle.

2. Tried a lidar tracker with stop-and-wait, later adding evasion (reversing).
   Why: stopping fails when a mover's path goes through the robot, as seen in the GUI and contact logs.
   Replaced by decision 4. Results are in the table below.

3. Tried a lidar-based RRT planner on top of the tracker.
   Why dropped: it was worse than wait/evade (7/20 against 14/20).

4. Gave the expert perfect obstacle information (teacher-student approach).
   Why: suggested by faculty as a choice to explore. It separates perception errors from decision errors.
   Risk: the MLP only sees lidar, so it may not fully match the expert. To be measured after training.

5. Used RRT in the expert, not in the MLP.
   Why: RRT is a planner that needs a world model and no training. The MLP learns from its demonstrations.

6. Chose RRT over reinforcement learning and Recovery RL for now.
   Why: the assignment centres on behaviour cloning, and RL would need far more simulator time.
   Recovery RL is a safety method, not a path finder. It remains a possible later extension.

7. Removed the tracking, planning and privileged options; kept only --rrt.
   Why: requested, to keep one implementation. SteerAround stays because RRTExpert uses it for parking and
   dagger.py and eval_obstacles.py use it.

8. Added a room boundary (--room HALF).
   Why: to bound the space. Walls count as obstacles. They are invisible in the GUI, which shows only a flat
   red rectangle on the floor, as requested. Movers turn round at the walls.

9. Removed waiting on scenes with no movers.
   Why: the robot waited about 10 s for no reason on static scenes.

10. Added the arm to the collision check (three small circles along the arm).
    Why: the logs showed collisions were not caused by drifting off the plan. The arm sticks out about 0.68 m
    in front of the base, past the 0.59 m the planner protected. Chosen over tucking the arm (which would
    change the reach and the training data) and over one big circle (which would block narrow gaps).

11. Raised the driving cutoff to 90 s for RRTExpert and limited reversing to 4 s per plan.
    Why: both timeouts came from the old 50 s cutoff plus slow plans (one plan had 29 s of reversing).
    A longer RRT search barely helped, so it was not used.
    Result: timeouts gone, but one new collision and slower mixed runs. Tuning results may be mostly plan randomness.

12. Run up to 4 RRT searches per plan and keep the earliest arrival.
    Why: reduces the effect of bad luck in the random search. Result: 60/60, runs over 50 s fell from 7 to 1.

13. Chose A* guidance, simple PyBullet boxes and our own collision checks for floor plans.
    Why: random search struggles with narrow doorways. OMPL had no documented support found for moving
    obstacles, and ready-made house datasets do not fit PyBullet simply.

14. Dot order: finish the current room first, taking the closest dot within it, then go to the next room.
    Why: chosen after GUI checks. Shortest total route left rooms early and walked past dots, which looked wrong.
    How: each room change adds 2.5 m to an order's cost. Among orders within 10% of the best, the one with the fewest room
    changes wins, then the one starting with the closest dot. Order is rechecked after each pick.
    Note: this is a strong preference, not a strict rule, so a much shorter route can still leave a room first.
    Cost: paths can be longer (fp_001: 88.2 -> 94.7 s).

15. Among similar routes, prefer the one that does not cross a moving obstacle's path (2 m penalty per crossing).
    Why: from a GUI check; the robot chose to pass a mover again when going on to another dot was about as short.

16. A tight route (under about 1.34 m) is allowed if it saves more than 1.5 m; within a room always take its closest dot.
    Why: GUI checks. The strict wide-route rule forced a 6.81 m route where 3.45 m was available.

17. Keep 0.2 m from movers in plans, and move to the safest nearby place when no plan exists, instead of freezing.
    Why: fair test found the robot frozen and pushed about 1 m by a mover (fp_021, 23 cm overlap): a plan passed a mover
    by 5 cm, the base fell behind it, and with no valid plan left the robot stood still.

18. MLP inputs are only: lidar history, its own pose and velocity, and the current dot's position. No A* and no map.
    Why: this is imitation learning. The expert may use perfect information, A* and RRT, but only to make demonstrations.
    Expected cost: finding a doorway from local lidar alone may be hard. To be measured.

19. Pure imitation for dot choice: the MLP gets the positions of all remaining dots and learns which to go to from the expert.
    This replaces "current dot's position" in decision 18. Why: no hand-written order logic in the learned policy.
    Risk: harder to train. The expert's order is mostly closest-first but depends on rooms and movers, which the MLP
    cannot fully see, and the MLP must learn to pick a target and hold it. To be measured.

20. The MLP outputs wheel commands only. Dots are not sorted by distance: they stay in their fixed scenario order.
    Why: chosen by the user, to keep the inputs exactly as agreed.

21. Data collection first, no training until the user says so. Keep only successful, contact-free runs, each at most 2 min.
    Five cases: open room without / with obstacles, hard floor plan without / with obstacles, hard plan + obstacles + 3 dots.
    Every 10th control step is saved (10 ms). Old test trajectories (39 GB) were deleted to free disk; summaries were kept.

22. MLP details: 199 inputs (5 lidar frames over the last 1 s, pose, velocity, 3 dot slots relative to the robot, each with a
    still-there flag). Outputs: 4 wheel speeds. Network: 3 layers of 256, batch 1024, Adam starting at 0.001.
    Why: matches decisions 18-20. The expert's current dot is left out so that dot choice is learned by imitation.
    Why a small network first: the larger one is about 2.5x slower per epoch, and the inputs are small.

23. Convergence is judged from validation loss over the last 10 epochs, plus the gap to train loss and the error per case.
    Why: a loss curve alone hides a hard case being learned badly. Learning rate halves after 4 flat epochs; training stops
    after 12 epochs without improvement; the best validation epoch is the one saved.
    The test split (82 runs) is kept unseen for the final evaluation.

24. DAgger with the RRT expert (dagger_mlp.py): the MLP drives the wheels while the expert keeps running, labels each state,
    and controls the arm, including when to reach. Each round drives 30 training scenarios per case, retrains from scratch on
    all data, and tests driving on the validation scenarios. Runs end early after 2 s of contact or 20 s without moving.
    Why: requested by the user, and the first model failed from states the expert rarely visits, such as standing still.
    Limit: only the wheels are learned, so the driving scores include expert help with the arm.

25. Train on the expert data only (runs/dataset, no DAgger) with input noise and left-right mirroring. Requested by the user.
    Noise: each training row gets its own strength, up to 0.05 m lidar, 0.03 m/s speed, 0.1 rad/s yaw rate. Half of each
    batch is mirrored afresh, so memory use does not grow. Validation stays clean and unmirrored, so results compare with runs/mlp1.
    Mirror check in the simulator, 3 scenes with mirrored wheel commands: lidar identical at the start; after 1.5 s the median
    difference was under 1 mm and only 2 of 1620 ray readings differed by over 5 cm, one in each of 2 scenes (likely rays
    grazing a box corner); robot state within about 2 cm.
    Limit: these target overfitting. The noise is small, so it may not stop the MLP copying its velocity. To be measured.

26. Train the arm as a separate MLP (train_arm_mlp.py), requested by the user: 24 arm inputs -> 7 torques, added to a physics
    gravity term, 2 layers of 128, on reaching rows only (527,618 training rows, median reach 2.6 s per dot).
    Why separate: the base is parked while the arm reaches, so reaching does not need the driving inputs, and each part can be tested alone.
    Why the gravity term: on joint 1 it explains most of the torque (expert RMSE 23.65 N m, 7.12 N m after removing gravity).
    Torque limits per joint: 1.25 x the 99.9th percentile of expert reach torques, at most 30 N m: 18.29, 30, 30, 30, 22.98, 30, 26.57.
    Expert labels and the MLP's torques are clipped to these. They are wide because the expert really does use brief large torques.
    The simulator's existing 30 N m limit still applies. No expert run or dataset was changed.
    Driving test: eval_arm_mlp.py. The expert still drives, parks, chooses the dot, decides when to reach, holds and retracts.

WHAT WE FOUND OUT
- User saw the robot circle a dot though it could reach it directly. Causes: only 4 straight-line closest spots were
  compared (some behind walls), and it had to reach its chosen spot exactly. Changes: compare all valid spots, and stop
  once within 0.2 m of reach distance with a clear line. On fp_022 and fp_011: arc around the dot 83-105 -> 20 deg or less.
- User GUI checks: odd dot order (cases 1, 5) and left-right shaking (cases 2, 3). Order chose the shortest total even by
  0.01 m and walked past dots. Shaking came from parking while facing away, near the start. Changes: room-change penalty
  (finish a room first), prefer the closest dot among near-equal orders, own parking turn. Fair test: 24/24.
- User saw an unnecessary turn-back in fp_005. Cause: A* accepted tight routes (down to 1.1 m) where the arm could not
  swing round, so RRT backed out and clipped furniture. Change: prefer routes with room to turn (0.67 m clearance, still
  fits 1.4 m doorways). On fp_005: no turn-back, contacts 233 -> 0, clearance 0 -> 20.7 cm. Retest: 24/24 clean.
- User saw the robot circling a dot before touching it. Cause: each dot had one fixed parking spot on a random side
  (26 of 72 legs went more than 60 deg around the dot, adding up to 2.7 m). Change: pick the valid spot on the
  shortest route. Result: 1 of 72 legs, total time 76.9 -> 67.9 s.
- With 3 dots, comparing all orders rarely beats going to the nearest dot. Too few cases to generalise.
- After reaching, the arm settles slightly off its starting pose (joint 6 at about 1.13 rad, not 1.00). The collision
  circles were measured for the starting pose. No collisions came from it in these tests.
- The base creeps slowly with its wheels stopped (about 0.5 m over 200 s). Small over a normal stop.
- A* guiding RRT through doorways works. Walls and furniture block the lidar, as the MLP will need.
- No mover collisions in the fair test, so the timing buffer and plan rejoining were skipped. Phase 1 is done.
- The one fair-test collision (traingmi_011) was the back-left wheel clipping a static obstacle for about 0.2 s while
  turning; the base was off its plan. Replanning sooner did not fix it and caused more replans, so it was reverted.
- Stopping is not enough: a mover whose path goes through the robot needs the robot to move away.
- Collisions came from the arm, not from drifting off the plan: 0-0.11 m off plan at contact, and only arm links
  touched in the two runs checked. Adding the arm to the collision check removed all 6 collisions.
- Timeouts came from a fixed 50 s driving cutoff plus slow plans. One plan used 29 s of reversing at 0.1 m/s.
- A longer RRT search barely helps (12x the search saved about 2 s), but running 4 separate searches does:
  mixed arrival fell from 42.8 to 31.7 s and runs over 50 s fell from 7 to 1.
- Route quality depends heavily on chance. Small code changes moved single runs by up to 15 s either way, so small
  before-and-after differences are unreliable.
- The old shorten() never improved a plan: rebuilding it changed its timing, causing collisions or later arrival.
  The new version keeps timing valid. Offline, plan length fell from 1.40 to 1.24 times the straight line, and
  planned arrival from 27.1 to 25.9 s. In the simulator it stayed 60/60, with shorter paths in all sets and only
  1 run over 40 s (was 4). Closest gap across all runs stayed about 7 cm.

  Train: 1461 runs, 5,154,384 rows. Validation: 82 runs, 288,264 rows. Stopped early after 17 of 60 epochs.
  Verdict: OVERFITTING. Best epoch 5 was kept: validation loss 0.0509, R2 0.949, wheel RMSE 0.269 rad/s.

FIRST DRIVING CHECK OF runs/mlp1 (7 validation runs, all five cases; not a full evaluation)
  0/7 successful, 0/11 dots. 4 timeouts, 2 stalled, 1 crashed. It barely moves, even in the easy empty room.
  Driving code checked: its commands match the training pipeline on saved rows to within 0.000005 rad/s, so this is not an input bug.
  Cause: the MLP mostly copies its current velocity instead of deciding where to go (known as the copycat or inertia problem).
    Velocity alone (linear fit) explains R2 0.898 of the wheel commands. Hiding velocity from the MLP: R2 0.947 -> -0.15.
    Hiding the dots: R2 unchanged. Robot still while the expert commands motion: expert 2.26 rad/s, MLP 0.06 rad/s.
  So the high R2 did not mean it learned to drive, and the overfitting was not the main problem.

KNOWN PROBLEMS
- The base can slide off its plan during turns and clip something close by (1 run in 88). Not fixed.
- Paths are still about 1.3-1.5 times the straight-line distance. Slowest run parks in 40-50 s.
- Plan quality varies a lot by chance: small code changes moved single static runs by up to 15 s either way.
- Some direction reversals remain (5 of 24 new runs). Not checked whether they are needed.
- Tight spots near movers can still cause brief contact (fp_014), and the base can clip walls (fp_007).
- The arm's reach is not collision-checked: targets very near a wall can make the fingers touch it (traingmi_001).
- Only tested with obstacle radius 0.15-0.30 m and mover speed 0.10-0.30 m/s.
- The arm circles assume the usual arm start pose (not arm_q0 scenarios).
- The arm's reach after parking and the last 0.3 m of parking are not checked against obstacles.
- The 60 scenarios were used to diagnose and fix problems, so a new set is needed for a fair final figure.
- The MLP cannot see the arm. After touching a dot it may creep instead of waiting for the arm to return. To be checked when driving.
- The first MLP ignores the dots and copies its velocity, so it cannot start moving. DAgger may reduce this; to be measured.

Data-size test, chosen by the user on 3 Nov: is lack of data causing the overfitting?
  Question: every training peaks at epoch 1 and then gets worse on validation. Two possible causes, not yet told apart:
    (a) the expert uses information the MLP does not get (map, A* route, RRT plan, dot order, best of 4 random plans), so similar inputs
        can have different correct commands, which the network can only memorise; (b) too few truly different examples: rows 10 ms
        apart are nearly identical, so 5.15 million rows are really about 1,461 runs.
  Test: same settings as mlp4-polar (no velocity, dot-polar, seed 0), trained on all, half and a quarter of the expert training runs.
    Validation runs unchanged. The subsets are fixed by scenario id and nested (the quarter is inside the half), with case proportions
    kept: all 1,461 runs, half 738, quarter 362. The full model is runs/mlp4-polar (best R2 0.600, epoch 1).
  Reading: much worse validation with less data means (b) matters and more data or regularisation should help. Little change means
    (a) dominates, and the fix is in the inputs or the expert, not in training settings.
  Result: best validation R2 0.600 with all runs (epoch 1), 0.487 with half (epoch 2), 0.457 with a quarter (epoch 1, so it peaked after
    only a quarter of the full model's updates). All three overfit straight after their peak; the half fell to 0.388 by epoch 12 while
    train loss fell from 0.27 to 0.08.
  Conclusion: less data clearly hurts, so lack of data is part of the problem, and regularisation or more varied data should help.
    The step from half to all (+0.11) was bigger than from quarter to half (+0.03), which is unusual for a learning curve. This is one seed and
    one subset each, so the exact sizes are uncertain. The full model's 0.60 ceiling also leaves room for the other cause (hidden expert
    information).
  Code: train_mlp.py --run-fraction.
    python train_mlp.py --out runs/size-half --no-velocity --dot-polar --run-fraction 0.5
    python train_mlp.py --out runs/size-quarter --no-velocity --dot-polar --run-fraction 0.25

DOOR MEMORY (feature set v2, added 3 Nov, user's choice: doorways only)
  Why: v1 doorway features come from the current scan only. A doorway is often seen clearly only up close, and is lost when
    the robot turns or backs away; far doorways get 2-3 rays. Remembering a doorway once seen keeps that information.
  Not a map and still imitation: a short list (at most 6) of doorway positions built only from the robot's own lidar and pose,
    reset every run. It gives facts, not decisions; training is still regression on the expert's wheel commands. Static
    obstacles are not remembered: the 360 degree lidar already sees them when they matter, and a mover mistaken for a still
    obstacle would leave a phantom in memory.
  How (preprocess.py remember()): each door-like opening (wall, gap, wall in line) is stored in world coordinates; sightings
    within 0.6 m of a stored doorway are averaged into it, with its wall direction. A doorway is confirmed (given to the MLP)
    after 3 scans and once a straight piece at least 1.0 m long has been seen beside it in some scan.
  New inputs (23, v1's 61 unchanged): number remembered; the nearest confirmed doorway (seen, distance, sin, cos, wall direction
    sin2/cos2, seconds since last seen); per dot the doorway whose wall line has the robot on one side and the dot on the other
    (else the one with the shortest way robot -> doorway -> dot): seen, distance, sin, cos, across (1/0).
  Check against the scenario truth (tests/check_door_memory.py, 10 runs per case, cases 3/4/5):
    rule                      true doorways remembered    remembered that are real   rows with a doorway
